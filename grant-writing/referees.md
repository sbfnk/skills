# Responding to referees, and to rejection

Adapted from Parker Derrington's catalogue. A separate task from writing the case; use it when reviews come back from the funder.

## Responding to referees

- Assume good intent. Even where it is untrue, the assumption improves the response.
- A misunderstanding is the writer's failure. Fix the text that allowed it before arguing the point.
- Thank the referees. Committees grade the response as well as the proposal.
- Restate each comment, then say exactly what changed, or why nothing did, with the location.
- Say how the feedback improved the project. The less changed, the more precisely the gain is described.
- Keep it short. Committees are time-pressed.
- Decline gracefully when a comment is unreasonable or uniformly lukewarm. A polite refusal beats forced compliance, and no response beats one with bad grace.

The response goes through the same show-then-apply loop as the proposal: each referee point becomes an item in a review record, with the proposed text in context, approved by the applicant before the file changes.

## Rejection

- Most material is salvageable. Treat the draft as inventory and the architecture file as its index.
- A rejection reflects the panel's mix, fit and timing as much as the idea. Do not over-update on one outcome.
- Decide quickly whether to resubmit, redirect to another funder or shelve. An application with no submission date never ends.
- Run the `retrospective` skill on the rejected application before starting the next one; the reviews go into `sources/` and are measured with the precedents at Step 0.
