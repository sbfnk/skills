# Briefs for fresh-context reviewers

A reviewer is a model run that has seen nothing of the writing sessions. It receives the render (PDF or the one-sentence-per-line source), the architecture file, the writing-rules section of the applicant file and one brief from this file. It never edits. It reports failures only, each located by paragraph label, with the sentence quoted. It does not propose rewrites unless the brief says so, and never praises.

## Filing and adjudication

The writer files the review in `sources/reviews/YYYY-MM-DD-<brief>-<vendor>.md` and writes an adjudication next to it: for each item, accepted, declined or deferred, and why, against the standing decisions in the architecture file. Two reviewers agreeing on a defect is the signal. Two agreeing that the text should stay is weaker. A reviewer asking for precision that narrows a programme to a project is noted and declined with the register decision cited.

At a decision point, put one question to two readers who cannot see each other's answer, as one command, both answers in one file.

## Standing briefs

**Specialist.** You know the nearest competing work. Where does the case claim novelty that the field would dispute? Which claim implies evidence the text does not cite? Which method choice would you challenge? Name the competing work you have in mind.

**Panel generalist.** You are a senior scientist outside this field on an interdisciplinary panel, with ten minutes. Where did you stop understanding? Which term is used without being introduced? Which paragraph did you skip? Which sentence did you read twice? What, in one sentence, is this project for?

**Sceptic.** You read for the scheme's hardest criterion, named here by the writer: ___. Where does the case claim to meet it without showing how? Which promise has no named people, data or time behind it? What is the weakest link in the argument from problem to work to outcome?

## Pass briefs

**Five-minute test.** Read the summary and the first page only (or, on a skeleton, the skeleton only). Answer the six questions in `derrington-checklist.md`, section 6. Report which you could not answer and which sentence you had to read twice.

**Seam pass.** Read the whole document once, for three things only. First, an argument interrupted by a point that belongs elsewhere: name the paragraph and where the point belongs. Second, a reference whose referent has moved or vanished: every "this", "these", "such", "those", "both", "the first", "the latter" in the paragraphs the writer lists as changed, with what it points at, or "nothing". Third, a sentence whose register differs from its paragraph. Nothing else.

**Diff review.** Here is today's diff and the whole render. Report inconsistencies the diff introduced: a statement now made two ways, a term now varied, a promise now unmatched, a number now disagreeing with another.

**Arc.** Does each paragraph follow from the one before? Does the ending answer the opening? Does each section's content match the funder's heading for it? Report the breaks.

**Referents.** Here is the list of paragraphs changed since the last pass. For every pronoun, demonstrative and "both" in them, say what it points at, or "nothing".

**Proportion.** Which example, disease, partner or study appears most often, and does its frequency match its role in the architecture? Are person and tense consistent across paragraphs? Which section is longer than its allocation and what in it is least needed?

**Promises audit.** List every "will" in the document. For each, say whether it depends on something in the applicant's hands and on staff the budget costs. Report the ones that do not.

**Claims audit.** List every sentence that implies evidence: a consensus attributed to a field, a global statement from one case, an assertion about current practice, a number. For each, the citation given, or "none". For a second vendor, this is the brief to use.

**Glossary pass.** Here is the tag-phrase list from the architecture file. Report every place a listed concept appears in other words.

**Whole-document assessment.** What is weakest, in order, and what would fix each item? A ranked list of at most seven items. No odds, no verdict, no praise.

**Narrowing check.** Here is a list of proposed review fixes and the register decision from the architecture file. Mark each fix that moves a programme towards a project, a question towards a hypothesis or an example towards a commitment.

## The applicant's own mock review

Ten minutes, in the voice of the least sympathetic likely reader, numbered. It is the only review that catches the jargon every model shares. The writer files it with the others and adjudicates it the same way.
